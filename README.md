Most "best course" lists rank courses without asking those questions. This page starts with your goal, then compares every course on the same facts.

Courses marked **Free** cost nothing. Prices change often, so check the course page before you buy.

## Best system design courses for interviews

### 1. Grokking the System Design Interview

[Grokking the System Design Interview](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-the-system-design-interview?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-interviews) is the original system design interview course. Arslan Ahmad, a former FAANG engineering manager, created it, and the "Grokking" approach to interview preparation began with this course. It teaches a repeatable 7-step method for open-ended questions. The method starts with clarifying the requirements and estimating the scale, then moves to the high-level design and a deep dive on the hardest component and its trade-offs.

The course then applies that method to the classic interview problems, including a URL shortener, Dropbox, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube, Uber, a web crawler, an API rate limiter, and Ticketmaster. Each problem has detailed diagrams, and most have a video walkthrough.

- **Format:** Text, diagrams, video walkthroughs, and quizzes.
- **Pricing:** One-time payment with lifetime access. The [annual plan](https://www.designgurus.io/pricing?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-interviews) includes every Design Gurus course.
- **Best for:** Most candidates preparing for a system design round at a large technology company.
- **Watch first:** [The official 7-step method video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N3r-Z6_IGTM), and a full [URL shortener walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_TsJizByBvE).
- **Pair it with:** The free [Grokking System Design repo](https://github.com/design-gurus/grokking-system-design), which adds study plans, cheat sheets, and company-specific guides.

### 2. Advanced System Design Interview, Volume II

[Advanced System Design Interview, Volume II](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-system-design-interview-ii?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-interviews) is the follow-up course for L5 and L6 candidates. Senior interviews reward depth over breadth, so this course explains how real distributed systems work and why they made their design choices. It includes deep dives into Dynamo, Kafka, and Cassandra, and hard problems such as payment and flash-sale systems.

- **Format:** Text, diagrams, and quizzes.
- **Pricing:** One-time payment with lifetime access, or included in the annual plan.
- **Best for:** Senior and staff candidates who already know the basic interview method.

### 3. System Design Interview Crash Course

[System Design Interview Crash Course](https://www.designgurus.io/course/system-design-interview-crash-course?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-interviews) is a 14-day course. It teaches a 14-step framework for analyzing a system and applies it to more than 30 real-world systems, so each day has a clear goal.

- **Best for:** Candidates with about two weeks before the interview.
- **If you have only a few days:** Use the free [1-week plan](https://github.com/design-gurus/grokking-system-design/blob/main/roadmaps/1-week-plan.md) and [system design in one page](https://github.com/design-gurus/grokking-system-design/blob/main/cheat-sheets/system-design-in-one-page.md).

### 4. ByteByteGo System Design Interview

[ByteByteGo's System Design Interview course](https://bytebytego.com/courses/system-design-interview/) is the online version of Alex Xu's *System Design Interview* books. It contains about 28 design chapters, from "Scale From Zero To Millions Of Users" to "Stock Exchange". The course is illustrated text. The well-known ByteByteGo videos are on its free YouTube channel, not in the paid course.

- **Pricing:** $189 per year or $499 lifetime at the current sale prices. ByteByteGo states that refunds are not available.
- **Best for:** Learners who already like the books and want the same material online.

### 5. Hello Interview

[Hello Interview](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/in-a-hurry/introduction) publishes written problem breakdowns, core concept pages, and deep dives on common technologies. Its system design section has 32 problem breakdowns, 16 of them free, and 24 of them include a video. Premium plans include AI-run practice interviews.

- **Pricing:** $47 for one month, $95 for one year, or $319 lifetime.
- **Best for:** Candidates who want written breakdowns of common problems. Its free "System Design in a Hurry" guide is one of the best free starting points.

### 6. Aced (formerly Exponent)

[Aced](https://www.aced.io/courses/system-design-interviews), the new name of Exponent, offers a system design course built around video lessons and mock interview recordings. The platform also offers peer mock interviews and AI feedback on practice answers.

- **Pricing:** Subscription plans. A free account is available.
- **Best for:** Learners who want video lessons and peer practice on the same platform.

### 7. AlgoMaster System Design Interviews

[AlgoMaster's System Design Interviews course](https://algomaster.io/learn/system-design-interviews), by Ashish Pratap Singh, has 113 written chapters, including 55 interview question designs. Chapters can be read as a book or played in an audio mode.

- **Pricing:** $39 per month, $99 per year, or $199 lifetime.
- **Best for:** Readers who want a large number of worked interview designs.

### 8. InterviewReady

[InterviewReady's System Design course](https://interviewready.io/course-page/system-design-course), taught by Gaurav Sen, has more than 35 video lessons. It builds an e-commerce application from one user to one million users, then covers networks, databases, consistency, messaging, and designs such as WhatsApp, Instagram, and Uber.

- **Pricing:** $18 per month, billed yearly, which includes every InterviewReady course.
- **Best for:** Learners who prefer video lectures and already follow Gaurav Sen's YouTube channel.

## Best system design courses for beginners

A beginner course should teach the building blocks before any interview problem: how databases, caches, load balancers, and message queues work, and what each one costs.

- [Grokking System Design Fundamentals](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-system-design-fundamentals?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-beginners) is the best structured starting point. It explains each building block in plain language, from databases to the CAP theorem, with a quiz at the end of every chapter. Take it before an interview course if terms such as sharding or replication are new to you.
- [Grokking Scalable Systems for Interviews](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-scalable-systems-for-interviews?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-beginners) shows how one system grows from a single server to many, one stage at a time. Each stage introduces one new component and explains why it became necessary.
- [Grokking Database Fundamentals for Tech Interviews](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-database-fundamentals-for-tech-interviews?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-beginners) covers storage, indexing, transactions, and replication. Every system design depends on these topics.
- [freeCodeCamp System Design Course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C842vFY5kRo) **Free**, by Hayk Simonyan, is a two-hour video on APIs, databases, caching, CDNs, and load balancing.
- [Design Software Systems Like a Senior Engineer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=soTfFFIQQ8U) **Free** is a longer freeCodeCamp video of about three hours.
- [NeetCode: System Design for Beginners](https://neetcode.io/courses/system-design-for-beginners/0) has 21 short lessons. The first two are free, and the rest require NeetCode Pro.

For a full beginner plan, read [System Design for Beginners: Where to Start](https://www.designgurus.io/blog/system-design-for-beginners?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-beginners) and [the best system design courses for beginners](https://www.designgurus.io/blog/best-system-design-courses-for-beginners?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-for-beginners).

## Best free system design courses

You can learn most of system design without paying. What free material usually lacks is a fixed order and worked interview answers, so choose one structured free source and follow it from start to finish.

- [Grokking System Design repo](https://github.com/design-gurus/grokking-system-design) **Free**: the open companion to the Grokking course. It includes system design patterns, worked interview questions, guides for more than 100 companies, paper deep dives, cheat sheets, and one-week to four-week study plans.
- [Hello Interview: System Design in a Hurry](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/in-a-hurry/introduction) **Free**: a compact written guide to the interview, plus 16 free problem breakdowns.
- [freeCodeCamp System Design Course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C842vFY5kRo) **Free**: a two-hour video introduction.
- [MIT 6.5840: Distributed Systems](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.824/) **Free**: the best-known university course on distributed systems. The labs build Raft and a sharded key-value store. The recorded lectures on the [course YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@6.824) are from an earlier semester, and the material is still current.
- [Cambridge: Concurrent and Distributed Systems](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/2122/ConcDisSys/materials.html) **Free**: Martin Kleppmann's lecture notes and videos on clocks, replication, and consensus.
- [CMU 15-445: Database Systems](https://15445.courses.cs.cmu.edu/) **Free**: Andy Pavlo's course on how databases work inside.
- [System Design Primer](https://github.com/donnemartin/system-design-primer) **Free**: the most-starred system design repository. It gives a broad overview, but it has no fixed study order.
- [System Design 101](https://github.com/ByteByteGoHq/system-design-101) **Free**: ByteByteGo's diagrams, each explaining one concept visually.
- [Design Gurus YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@designgurus1173) **Free**: short videos on caching, sharding, Kafka, APIs, and full interview walkthroughs. See [free system design videos](#free-system-design-videos).
- [Design Gurus free system design guides](https://www.designgurus.io/learn-system-design?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-free-system-design-courses) **Free**: concept lessons and interview questions, each in plain language.

See also [10 free resources to learn system design](https://www.designgurus.io/blog/free-system-design-resources?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-free-system-design-courses).

## Best system design courses on Udemy

Udemy courses are video courses with frequent sales. They are a good introduction, but they are shorter than the dedicated interview courses above and include fewer worked interview problems.

- [Mastering the System Design Interview](https://www.udemy.com/course/system-design-interview-prep/) by Frank Kane (Sundog Education): about 5 hours of video in 73 lectures, rated 4.6 from more than 15,000 ratings.
- [Software Architecture & Design of Modern Large Scale Systems](https://www.udemy.com/course/software-architecture-design-of-modern-large-scale-systems/) by Michael Pogrebinsky: about 8 hours of video in 52 lectures, rated 4.7 from more than 19,000 ratings. It focuses on architecture concepts more than on interview problems. A similarly named course at a different URL is by another instructor, so check the instructor name before you buy.

If you want lifetime access outside Udemy with more interview problems, [Grokking the System Design Interview](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-the-system-design-interview?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-system-design-courses-on-udemy) is a one-time purchase.

## University and Coursera courses

- [Software Design and Architecture Specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/software-design-architecture) (University of Alberta, Coursera): four courses, about 52 hours in total. It covers design principles, design patterns, and architecture styles. It is part of Coursera Plus, and Coursera offers financial aid.
- [Software Architecture](https://www.coursera.org/learn/software-architecture) (University of Alberta, Coursera): the architecture course from that specialization, about 10 hours, available on its own.
- [Cloud Computing Specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/cloud-computing) (University of Illinois, Coursera): the distributed systems theory behind cloud services.

University courses teach software design well. They do not teach the interview format, so add an interview course if you have an interview coming.

## Best courses for senior and staff engineers

Senior and staff loops expect you to drive the discussion, choose between trade-offs, and explain how a design fails. A beginner course will not prepare you for that.

- [Advanced System Design Interview, Volume II](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-system-design-interview-ii?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-courses-for-senior-and-staff-engineers): the depth that L5 and L6 interviews expect.
- [System Design Patterns: From Fundamentals to Real Systems](https://www.designgurus.io/course/system-design-patterns?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=best-courses-for-senior-and-staff-engineers): 93 lessons on the recurring patterns, such as write-ahead logs, leader election, and quorums, shown inside production systems.
- [MIT 6.5840: Distributed Systems](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.824/) **Free**: the theory behind those patterns.
- [Senior and staff refresher](https://github.com/design-gurus/grokking-system-design/blob/main/roadmaps/senior-staff-refresher.md) **Free**: a plan for experienced engineers who have not interviewed in several years.

For books and papers at this level, see the [books](https://github.com/design-gurus/best-system-design-resources#books) and [papers](https://github.com/design-gurus/best-system-design-resources#papers-every-engineer-should-read) sections of Best System Design Resources.

## How to choose a system design course

Answer these four questions in order.

1. **How soon is your interview?** About two weeks: take the [Crash Course](https://www.designgurus.io/course/system-design-interview-crash-course?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=how-to-choose-a-system-design-course). Four to eight weeks: take [Grokking the System Design Interview](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-the-system-design-interview?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=how-to-choose-a-system-design-course). No interview planned: start with [Fundamentals](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-system-design-fundamentals?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=how-to-choose-a-system-design-course) or MIT 6.5840.
2. **Do you know the building blocks?** If you cannot explain caching, sharding, and replication in one sentence each, take a beginner course first.
3. **What level is the role?** For L5 and above, add [Volume II](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-system-design-interview-ii?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=how-to-choose-a-system-design-course) after the main course.
4. **Text or video?** Text courses are faster to review before an interview. Video courses suit learners who need to hear an explanation. Many courses now mix both.

Whatever you choose, finish one course before you start a second. Most candidates who fail the round have read parts of many courses and practiced none of them aloud.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best system design course?

For most interview candidates, [Grokking the System Design Interview](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-the-system-design-interview?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=frequently-asked-questions). It teaches one repeatable method and applies it to the problems that interviewers ask most often. Beginners should take [Grokking System Design Fundamentals](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-system-design-fundamentals?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=frequently-asked-questions) first, and senior candidates should add [Volume II](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-system-design-interview-ii?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=frequently-asked-questions).

### What is the best free system design course?

For interview preparation, the [Grokking System Design repo](https://github.com/design-gurus/grokking-system-design) and Hello Interview's free [System Design in a Hurry](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/in-a-hurry/introduction). For theory, [MIT 6.5840](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.824/). For a first video, the [freeCodeCamp System Design Course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C842vFY5kRo).

### Is Grokking the System Design Interview worth it?

For candidates interviewing at large technology companies, yes. It is a one-time purchase with lifetime access, and the method it teaches applies to problems the course does not cover. If you only want a broad overview with no interview coming, a free resource is enough. Read the [comparison of system design interview courses](https://www.designgurus.io/blog/best-system-design-interview-courses-2026?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=frequently-asked-questions) for a longer answer.

### ByteByteGo or Grokking: which is better?

They suit different learners. ByteByteGo is illustrated text based on Alex Xu's books, sold as a yearly or lifetime subscription. Grokking the System Design Interview teaches a step-by-step interview method, adds video walkthroughs to most problems, and is a one-time purchase. Candidates who want a method to follow in the room usually prefer Grokking.

### What is the best system design course on Udemy?

[Mastering the System Design Interview](https://www.udemy.com/course/system-design-interview-prep/) by Frank Kane for a short interview introduction, and [Software Architecture & Design of Modern Large Scale Systems](https://www.udemy.com/course/software-architecture-design-of-modern-large-scale-systems/) by Michael Pogrebinsky for architecture concepts. Both are shorter than the dedicated interview courses.

### Which system design course does Reddit recommend?

Reddit threads most often mention Grokking the System Design Interview, Alex Xu's books and ByteByteGo, Hello Interview's free guides, the System Design Primer, and *Designing Data-Intensive Applications*. The common advice is to pick one structured course, add one deep book, and practice aloud.

### How long does it take to learn system design?

Most engineers need four to eight weeks of steady study to prepare for an interview. Engineers with production distributed systems experience often need less. See [how long it takes to prepare](https://www.designgurus.io/blog/how-long-to-prepare-for-system-design-interview?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=best-system-design-courses&utm_content=frequently-asked-questions) for a breakdown by experience level.

### Should I take a course or read a book?

Do both, for different purposes. A course gives you an order and worked interview answers. A book such as *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* explains why the patterns work. If you have time for only one, take the course when an interview is coming and read the book when it is not.
